## 1. The strategy in one paragraph

Stop offering "extra hands". Pick the job types that pay and that fit 15 years of WooCommerce, WordPress and Shopify work. Build practice demos for each type and publish each 60–90 second walkthrough on LinkedIn, YouTube and a Pervej.com Facebook Page. Send a video only to people who have shown they have that problem now: a job post, a reaction to a demo, a profile view, or a warmed agency lead whose services match. Run 500 such tries and judge the result against a pass mark fixed today: one paid task started. Upwork stays what it is in every other file: the place the topics come from, read-only.

## 2. What the job posts show

**The sample.** 119 job posts from the second export: fixed price, $2,000 or more, clients in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, newest first. Three searches: "shopify" (32 posts), "wordpress" (37 after removing 5 repeats) and "ai integration" (50 of 68 listed).

| # | Job type | Posts | Median budget | At $5,000+ | Already 50+ proposals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store or site build, redesign or theme work from a design | 39 | $2,500 | 8 | 30 of 39 |
| 2 | Operations automation with an AI step (CRM, email, Sheets, Slack) | 12 | $3,000 | 5 | 12 of 12 |
| 3 | AI product or SaaS build, first version | 11 | $5,000 | 7 | 7 of 11 |
| 4 | Custom commerce logic and portals (price calculators, product configurators, B2B dealer portals, member and booking systems) | 11 | $3,750 | 5 | 6 of 11 |
| 5 | Audit, rescue and hardening (AI-assisted builds, pre-launch checks, speed, accessibility, AI accuracy) | 7 | $4,000 | 3 | 5 of 7 |
| 6 | AI image or content tools | 5 | $4,000 | 2 | 5 of 5 |
| 7 | Assistant that answers from the client's own documents (three of the four require citations, refusals or a test set) | 4 | $4,000 | 2 | 4 of 4 |
| 8 | Voice, SMS and booking agents for service businesses | 4 | $4,000 | 2 | 4 of 4 |
| 9 | Platform migration with search rankings preserved (one job posted twice) | 4 | $2,000 | 0 | 2 of 4 |
| 10 | Store integrations and sync (Amazon stock, payment gateways, referral systems) | 3 | $2,500 | 0 | 1 of 3 |

The other 19 posts: 9 hires posted as fixed price, 2 consulting-only, 1 conversion-rate project, 2 "connect an AI assistant to my tools or data", 5 vague, placeholder or non-development.

**Three readings**

1. **Most common is not best paid.** Builds and redesigns are 39 of the 119 posts with a $2,500 median. Every type with a median of $4,000 or more is something else. The first export agrees on the share: 77 of its 142 posts were builds or design.
2. **In store work, custom logic and audits pay more than builds.** Custom logic has a $3,750 median and 5 of its 11 posts at $5,000 or more; builds have $2,500 and 8 of 39. The four audits in the store searches have a $3,250 median. Stand-alone integration jobs paid the same as builds ($2,500, 3 posts).
3. **AI work has more large jobs, is as crowded as WordPress, and is rarely about stores.** 21 of the 50 AI posts were at $5,000 or more, against 15 of the 69 store posts. 42 of the 50 already had 50+ proposals, the same share as WordPress (31 of 37); Shopify was 15 of 32. Only 3 of the 50 involved selling products at all, and only one of those, a wholesaler with a $12,000 budget, asked for this kind of automation. About a third asked for proof: a measured accuracy figure, a test set, a human approval step or a log.

**Who posts.** About one post in eight comes from an agency, studio or reseller: 14 clear cases, up to 18 with the borderline ones. Six of the 14 are builds, and three are the agency's own hiring or back office. The rest are the businesses themselves.

## 3. The five job types to target

Chosen first by fit (what Yeasir can already show, or is learning), then by pay, then by what these buyers ask to see. Proposal counts on Upwork play no part, because nothing is bid there.

| # | Job type | Why it is on the list | What the demo must prove, in measured numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Custom commerce logic and portals** | The best-evidenced fit: 11 posts, $3,750 median, 5 at $5,000+. Already S3 and S5 on the service menu; member-portal and plugin work in the history | Every pricing or access case listed and passing · the client edits rates from the admin, no code · a real test order at the edge cases |
| T2 | **Audit and rescue of AI-assisted builds** | 7 posts in the wider audit type, 3 of them about an AI-assisted build ($2,500–4,000). It is the review Yeasir already does on every Claude Code diff, sold as a service | Findings by severity, each with evidence · every fix retested · speed before and after · a launch or don't-launch note |
| T3 | **Store integrations and sync** | Small on its own (3 posts, paid like builds), but it is S4, and it is the connecting work inside T1 and T4 jobs | A stated number of replayed events, including duplicates and out-of-order ones, with zero double counts · a failed call retried on screen · a reconciliation report |
| T4 | **Operations automation with an AI step, inside a store business** | The largest type after builds: 12 posts, 5 at $5,000+. A new skill on home ground: orders, customers, the support inbox | A test set in the real message format, accuracy as correct out of total · a human approval step · a log of every action · cost per 1,000 messages |
| T5 | **Assistant that answers from the store's own documents, cites the source or refuses** | 4 posts; two ask for it in near-identical words. Maps to the "grounded chat" and "evals" items of the AI-commerce catalog | A 50-question acceptance set split into right with source, correctly refused, and wrong · a hard spend cap |

T4 and T5 are a bet. The AI search holds more of the $5,000+ jobs, and these two types build the skills Yeasir is adding. But no online store in the sample asked for either; the closest is one wholesaler. If neither has drawn a single "send me the link" by the verdict in §4.5, they stay as training and leave the outreach.

**Not targets**

- **Builds and redesigns.** Take them when asked. They are the most common type, the lowest paid of the large ones, and in Claude's judgment the most exposed to AI-assisted building. Never the headline, and a demo topic only when the build carries one of the five.
- **Migrations.** Done before. The stand-alone posts listed $2,000–2,500; posts where migration was part of a larger scope listed $5,000–5,500. Show one inside a bigger demo.
- **"Connect an AI assistant to my tools or data" (MCP).** Two posts of that kind; MCP is named in five posts overall. Build it as the delivery form of T4, not as a separate offer.
- **Skip:** AI product builds (best paid, but a different kind of work: whole products in Next.js, TypeScript, Python and Postgres), voice and SMS agents, AI image tools, hires.

## 4. How to win them

### 4.1 Demos

Each demo runs through the demo plan and the runbook as they stand: brief in the client's voice, questions, plan, real timestamps, QA, handoff, the practice-build label. Every demo is set in a WooCommerce or Shopify store, so gate G1 holds. Three gates change (decision 2):

- G2: "a task an agency hands off" becomes "a task a business or an agency hands off" (follows decision 1).
- G3: free tiers and sandboxes count as "what we have": self-hosted n8n, a free CRM tier, a second dev store.
- G5: the AI demos get two weeks each instead of eight hours.

Yeasir builds the AI demos himself, because those are the skills he is adding. The store demos follow the demo plan's roles.

| Video live (Thu) | Demo | Type | Trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 Oct | Pre-launch audit of an AI-assisted WooCommerce build | T2 | A repeatable audit checklist: security, speed, checkout, tracking |
| 22 Oct | Price-by-area calculator the client can edit from the admin | T1 | Custom pricing logic, admin-editable settings |
| 29 Oct | Instagram and Messenger inbox agent for a WooCommerce store, answering from live store data, with a 50-message test set | T4 | LLM tool calling inside a WordPress plugin, Meta messaging webhooks, building a test set |
| 5 Nov | Stock sync between two stores that survives duplicate events | T3 | Webhooks, retries, reconciliation |
| 12 Nov | The same agent's search step: it finds products and policy answers by meaning, and cites its source or refuses | T5 | Retrieval, acceptance tests, spend caps |
| 19 Nov | Dealer-only pricing and ordering | T1 | Roles, tiered pricing, gated catalogues |
| Next | Support-inbox triage for a store, with a 50-message test set (moved from 29 Oct) | T4 | Python, direct LLM API calls, n8n |
| Next | Weekly client report for an agency, built from store data and approved before it sends | T4 | The same skills, aimed at the agency leads |
| Next | MCP server over store and Customer History data: read-only, approval before any write | T4 | MCP; also the first piece of the Customer History AI add-on |

Each is modelled on a post in the two exports already sent, so the briefs can be issued without a new feed. The AI demos move a non-store post into a store setting. The demo plan allows that: a demo recreates the category of problem, never the poster's own case. If the Woo checkout demo from the earlier plan is already under way, publish it first and move this list back a week.

**The 29 Oct and 12 Nov demos are one build, and the exception to that rule** (decision 8). They are the AI inbox agent that Launch My Boutique sells, built on its demo store and shown here as a practice build. The brief is the build spec in the Launch My Boutique project's inbox-agent strategy, not a job post. The video shows the skill and does not mention Launch My Boutique. It still has to show the T4 and T5 proof in §3: answers right out of 50, the handoff cases, a log of every action, and cost per 1,000 messages. The support-inbox triage demo that held the 29 Oct slot, with its Python and n8n practice, moves to "Next".

### 4.2 Publishing

- **LinkedIn, Thursday:** the native video exactly as the video guideline says, on Yeasir's own profile.
- **YouTube, same day:** the same file. Title is the problem line; description is the label and pervej.com. A square video under three minutes is published as a Short. YouTube is a host and a search listing, not a lead channel, so it gets no content of its own.
- **Facebook Page, same day:** a Page named Pervej.com, with the same file and the same post text. Facebook publishes every video as a Reel. The Page is a shelf, not a channel: no content of its own, no follower-growth work, no targets. It is there so the videos and the Page exist when a paid Meta test starts. It stays separate from the Launch My Boutique pages.
- **One page per demo on pervej.com** (`/demos/<id>`): the problem line, the video, what was measured, the label, the approved ask. This is the link that gets sent. Not `portfolio.pervej.com`, which stays untouched and unlinked.
- **Check pervej.com loads.** On 5 Oct a fetch from Claude's side returned an SSL error (526). Open it in a private window before any link goes out.

### 4.3 Profile

- **Headline now:** *Senior WooCommerce & Shopify Developer | Custom Logic, Integrations & Pre-Launch Audits | pervej.com*. This drops "for Marketing Agencies", which decision 1 now allows.
- **Headline after the first AI demo is public with its accuracy figure:** add *Tested AI Automation* after *Audits*. Not before.
- **About:** the first two lines name the five problems. The white-label terms stay on the site for agencies.
- **Featured:** the three best demo videos, pervej.com, Customer History.
- **"Providing services":** describe the five types in its text.
- **Unchanged:** no Upwork name, no client names, nothing coach-related, no result that was not measured.

### 4.4 Outreach on triggers

The 12 requests a day stay. What changes is who gets them and what they say.

| Trigger | Who | Order |
|---|---|---|
| A LinkedIn job post (contract, part-time or freelance) for one of the five types, at an agency or a company under about 50 people | The poster, hiring manager or founder | First |
| A reaction to a demo post, or a profile view, from someone who fits | That person | Second |
| A warmed agency lead whose services include the demo's topic | The owner or operations lead | Fills the rest |

**Sequence**

1. Connection request that names the trigger. No link, no pitch. Never mention that they viewed the profile.
   > Saw [Company] is looking for help with [stock sync between Shopify and Amazon]. I built a practice version of exactly that last week. Glad to connect.
2. On acceptance, one message:
   > I recorded a 90-second walkthrough of that practice build: what breaks, how I tested it, and what gets handed over. Want the link?
3. On a yes, the demo page link and the ask. For a job poster: *"If you would rather have this delivered than wait for a hire, send me the brief."* For everyone else, the approved ask: *"When the next one lands, message me."*
4. Follow-ups at +4 and +10 days as already set, then stop.

**Rules**

- A practice build is always called a practice build.
- Yeasir is never a candidate for the job; he offers a delivery.
- Yeasir holds every conversation. The VA finds the triggers and logs them.
- InMail only for a job poster who cannot be reached by a connection request.
- Nobody found through an Upwork post is ever looked up or contacted anywhere else.
- New sheet columns: `trigger` · `demo sent` · `asked for video`.

### 4.5 The 500-try rule

A try is one connection request or InMail sent on a named trigger, counted from the day the first demo video is live. The first 50 requests used the old pitch and do not count. At 12 each weekday from Thu 15 Oct, the 500th goes out about Fri 11 Dec. Each read waits until the tries it covers have had their follow-ups.

| Read on | Covers | Expect | If not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon 16 Nov (the existing verdict date) | The first 150 tries, sent by 2 Nov | 38 or more accepted (25%) and at least 5 people asking for a video | Change the message once and tighten the triggers |
| Mon 30 Nov | The first 300, sent by 18 Nov | At least 2 calls or scoping threads | Change which demo leads; keep the channel |
| Mon 28 Dec | All 500 | At least 1 paid task started | Stop outbound requests. Keep publishing and answering inbound |

The "expect" figures are planning assumptions built on the funnel in the ad-strategy file, not promises. Read each one per trigger: one trigger can be dropped while another carries on. If the first video slips, every date slips with it.

### 4.6 Paid reach

Unchanged from the ad plan. Nothing until four or more Thursday videos are out and at least two drew reactions from buyers. Then Thought Leader Ads at $10 a day through a Pervej.com Company Page, sponsoring the posts that already sit on Yeasir's profile. No lead-gen forms.

**Meta ads:** nothing paid for now. A $100 test of a fixed-price pre-launch audit offer was proposed on 5 Oct and is not decided.

## 5. Upwork

- Constraint C1 and operating principle 1 stand: read-only, no bidding, no messaging, no profile activity for this venture. This strategy does not need bidding.
- If Yeasir ever wants to change that, it is a strategy-doc change first, not a step in a content plan. Upwork's own rule would then apply to every proposal and every linked page: any contact route before a contract, including a LinkedIn handle, is a violation, with penalties up to permanent loss of the account. Work-sample links are allowed. The published demo videos sit on LinkedIn and link to pervej.com, so they could not be the link.
- Recommendation: leave it. 88 of the 119 posts already had 50+ proposals, and the downside is the profile the salary runs through.

## 8. Method and limits

- **Second export:** as described in §2. Repeats across the three searches were removed. One migration job that its client posted twice counts as two posts.
- **First export:** 142 posts from "shopify", "woocommerce" and "wordpress" searches, budgets from $500, hourly work included, sorted by Upwork's personalised "best match". It shares 12 posts with the second export and was sorted into different categories, so it is used here only for the share of builds.
- **Job types** are Claude's reading of each title and description. A second, independent pass over all 119 posts agreed on 110. Five labels were changed as a result and four borderline ones were left, so any count can move by one or two. An earlier chat estimate of "about 44" builds among the 69 store posts is now 39.
- **Budgets** are the listed figures, and some are nominal. Most types have 3 to 12 posts, so the medians are indications. Four posts at $100,000 or more were treated as placeholders and left out of the budget figures.
- **Upwork's own search counters** showed 29, 15 and 4 jobs at $5,000 or more for the three searches. Those include placeholders, repeats and the 18 AI posts that were not in the export, so §2 uses the posts actually captured.
- **Proposal counts** are Upwork's brackets on the day of export.
- That builds are the type most exposed to AI-assisted building is a judgment from the earlier research in this chat. The exports do not measure it.
- Upwork shows what is asked for and the budgets attached. It does not show what a buyer reached on LinkedIn will pay. §4.5 is how that gets measured.
