## 1. Concept

### The 2026 headline — B2B is no longer Plus-only

The ground moved five months ago, and it moved in your favor. **Since April 2026** (official changelog), native B2B runs on **Basic, Grow, and Advanced** at no extra cost: company profiles, **up to 3 active B2B catalogs (assigned via Markets)**, volume pricing, payment terms, vaulted credit cards, ACH (US). **Plus keeps:** unlimited catalogs, **direct catalog assignment to companies and locations** (non-Plus routes assignment through Markets), partial payments and deposits, and the heavier operational tooling (rep workflows, registration/quoting flows remain gaps or Plus/app territory).

Scoping consequences, T4.1-style — plan is the first question:

- **Non-Plus client, ≤3 pricing tiers, modest buyer count** → native B2B now fits; the app stack many of them run is suddenly optional (a migration conversation you can start).
- **The tells that mean Plus (or apps):** 4+ price tiers · catalogs needing per-company assignment rather than Markets routing · deposits/partial payments · sales-rep ordering · self-serve wholesale registration at scale. Rough scale line the market uses: tens of wholesale accounts run fine on standard plans; hundreds push toward Plus.
- Your dev store previews the full (Plus-grade) feature set — build there, scope per the client's real plan.

### The data model — entities, not role flags

This is T1.5's lesson at business scale. Woo's wholesale world hung everything off a user role; Shopify models the actual business:

```
Company ("Padma Trading Co")
├── Location: Dhaka HQ          ← the buying entity: its own catalogs,
│     terms, addresses, tax IDs    payment terms, checkout behavior
├── Location: Chattogram Branch ← different catalog, different flow
└── Contacts (people) — per-location permissions:
      who can order, who admins the account
```

A buyer belongs to a company, acts *at a location*, and everything prices and behaves per location. One person can buy for multiple locations (the storefront grows a **location picker**). Get this model into your bones — every configuration below hangs off it.

### Catalogs — the pricing machine

A **catalog** = a price list + a publication, attached to buyers:

- **Prices:** an overall % adjustment off list, and/or **fixed prices** per variant (fixed beats percentage where both exist).
- **Publication:** which products the catalog's buyers can see/buy at all — assortment control, not just pricing.
- **Quantity rules:** per-variant min / max / **increments** ("sold in packs of 6").
- **Volume pricing:** quantity price breaks (10+ pays X, 50+ pays Y).
- **Assignment:** direct to company locations on Plus; **via Markets on non-Plus** — same engine, different routing; know both shapes for scoping.

### Terms, checkout, and the two order flavors

Per location: **payment terms** (Net 7/15/30/…, due on receipt or fulfillment), **vaulted cards**, ACH (US), **PO number** capture — and the flow switch that matters operationally: **checkout to draft**, where the buyer's order lands as a draft for merchant review before it becomes real. Two flavors, both in today's drill: *pay/terms at checkout* and *submit for review*.

### The buyer experience — and the theme layer

B2B runs on **new customer accounts** (T4.1's fork, resolved for wholesale: there's no classic-accounts B2B). A contact logs in (email code), and the *same storefront* flips contextually: their prices render, the location picker appears, terms surface at checkout. No separate store, no duplicate theme — **blended** DTC+B2B on one store is the standard mid-market shape now (Plus expansion stores exist for dedicated setups).

The theme craft — where S5 stops being configuration and starts being your work:

- Contextual pricing renders automatically; **volume-pricing tables and quantity-rule messaging** need theme support (current Dawn ships it — verify on your version).
- **Quick order list** — Dawn's B2B-oriented section: variant-grid bulk entry, the wholesale UX centerpiece.
- **Gated content:** sections that render only for B2B buyers (welcome banners, trade info, price-list downloads) via the B2B context objects — `customer.b2b?`, `customer.current_company`/location objects (toolkit-verify exact names at build time; this corner of Liquid is newer and drifts).

### The gaps — a.k.a. the custom-work menu

Native B2B has honest holes, and every hole is S5 revenue:

1. **No public wholesale application form** — buyers are added manually. The classic custom build: storefront application form → middleware → Admin API company/contact creation → approval flow. (Your T5.2 skills, pointed at the highest-value client type. This is the S5 flagship project.)
2. **No real RFQ/negotiation** — draft-order workflows and apps fill it.
3. **Rep ordering / hierarchies** — Plus tooling and apps territory.
4. **Non-Plus:** the 3-catalog cap, Markets-routed assignment, no B2B in POS.

The S5 pitch anatomy, decomposed: **native config** (fast, high-leverage) **+ theme B2B UX** (your Module 1–2 skills) **+ custom glue** (forms, integrations, Functions) — that's the 1–3 week project the service menu prices.

|---|---|---|
| `wholesale_customer` role + Wholesale Suite / B2BKing license stack | Companies, locations, contacts — **native entities** | Another license line item deleted; the model is richer than roles ever were |
| Role-based pricing rules | Catalogs (% + fixed + publication) | Assortment control included, not bolted on |
| Min/max/step quantity plugins | Quantity rules, per variant, native | — |
| Tiered-pricing table shortcodes | Volume pricing, theme-rendered | The table is platform data now |
| Request-a-quote plugins | Checkout-to-draft review flow | True negotiation is still a gap — say so |
| Net terms via BACS + manual invoicing gymnastics | Native terms + vaulted cards + ACH | Dunning-grade plumbing, built in |
| Registration/approval plugins | **The gap** — manual adds | And therefore your flagship custom build |
| The "separate wholesale store" hack | One blended store, contextual per login | The hack retires; the theme does the flipping |

## 3. Claude Code drill — a working wholesale scenario, both flavors

Branch `t6-1-b2b` for theme work; most of today lives in admin. Screenshot *everything* — this run is S5 proof material and demo-store rehearsal in one.

### Part A — Model the business (~30 min)

Admin → **Companies**: create **Padma Trading Co** with two locations — **Dhaka HQ** and **Chattogram Branch** (real-ish addresses). Add yourself as a contact with ordering permission at both, admin at HQ. Confirm new customer accounts are on (Settings → Customer accounts) — B2B requires them.

### Part B — Catalog craft (~40 min)

1. **Catalog "Wholesale −30%":** 30% off list overall · **fixed price** on one hero SKU (prove fixed-beats-percentage) · **volume pricing** on that SKU (10+ and 50+ breaks) · **quantity rules**: min 6, increments of 6. Assign to **Dhaka HQ**.
2. **Catalog "Preferred −15%":** simple 15% adjustment → **Chattogram Branch**.
3. Note in the b2b-map file: on a non-Plus client this assignment routes **via Markets** and you'd budget the 3-catalog cap — write the sentence now, while the difference is on screen.

### Part C — Terms and the two flows (~20 min)

- **Dhaka HQ:** payment terms **Net 30** · PO numbers on · standard checkout.
- **Chattogram Branch:** **checkout to draft** (submit for review).

### Part D — The buyer walkthrough (~60 min, the money part)

Log in as your contact through new customer accounts (email code). Then walk both flavors, screenshots throughout:

1. **The flip:** storefront now shows Padma's context — find the **location picker**, set **Dhaka HQ**.
2. **The hero product:** −30% pricing rendered · the **volume-pricing table** · quantity rules live — try qty 4, watch the enforcement to 6; try 7, watch increments.
3. **Flow 1 (HQ):** cart at 12 units (volume break visible) → checkout → **Net 30 offered, PO field present** → place it → open the order in admin: terms, due date, PO all on the record.
4. **Flow 2 (Chattogram):** switch location — *watch the prices change on the same products* — checkout → **submitted for review** → find the **draft** in admin → review, finalize → real order.

That location-switch price change is the demo moment that sells S5 to agencies. Frame the screenshot well.

### Part E — The theme pass (~60 min)

1. Verify Dawn's rendering of volume tables and rule messaging on your version; note anything missing (older client themes often lack it — that's a line-item).
2. Add the **Quick Order List** section on a page or collection; bulk-enter a mixed order as the buyer. Wholesale UX centerpiece, witnessed.
3. **One custom build:** a small `b2b-welcome` section — gated on the B2B context, greeting `company` + current location by name, with a merchant-editable message (schema muscle). Toolkit-verify the exact context objects before prompting; review protocol as always. DTC visitors must never see it — prove by logging out.

### Part F — Map, gaps, and the pitch (~30 min)

`notes/b2b-map.md`, three blocks:

1. **Coverage table** with a plan column: what you configured today · non-Plus shape (3 catalogs, Markets routing, no POS) · Plus exclusives (unlimited, direct assignment, deposits/partials).
2. **The gaps → custom menu:** the application-form flow **spec'd in five lines** (form → middleware → Admin API create → notify → approve) as the flagship S5 custom project; RFQ honesty; rep-tooling honesty.
3. **Scoping anatomy:** config + theme UX + custom glue, with the scale-line sentence and the migration angle (non-Plus clients paying for wholesale apps that April 2026 made optional).

Productization line: *wholesale application-form flow — extraction candidate on repeat #3.* Commit branch + notes.
